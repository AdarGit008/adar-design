## Overview

This mode is a Commodore 64 powered on: a 40-column by 25-row PETSCII screen of light-blue bitmap characters on deep blue, surrounded by the machine's bright blue border. Everything quantizes to the 8x8 hardware cell, so type, rules, panels, and pixel art all share one grid instead of being optically nudged. The register is chunky, flat, and slightly awkward on purpose — a home computer, not a workstation. There is no gloss anywhere: color comes from sixteen fixed attributes, and depth comes from inverting them or from a hard offset block.

## Colors

The ground is the classic C64 blue #0000aa with #a0a0ff light-blue text, #c6c6ff for bright values and the block cursor, and #6a6ab8 as the dim attribute; panels lift one step to #2e2c9b. The signature frame uses the bright blue #3f3fff with a #2f2fff inner edge, which is deliberately a non-text tone — it separates the screen from the page the way the VIC-II border does on the television. Red #c46c71, yellow #edf171, and green #a9ff9f exist only as attribute signals and never as surface fills, and every one of them comes from the sixteen-entry Pepto palette in paletteVariants.c64Pepto rather than from a modern hex.

## Typography

C64 Pro Mono is the whole voice — headings, buttons, status lines, form values, and body copy — because the machine had exactly one character set and hierarchy came from color and case. Headings are UPPERCASE and may scale to 2x or 3x the 8x8 cell (16/32/48px) with image-rendering: pixelated, while body copy stays at 16px/16px to keep the glyph grid intact. No italic, faux bold, or wide tracking is faked: letter-spacing tops out at 0.05em so the cell never shifts. Pet Me 64, VT323, and Courier New are the degradation path, and the stack always ends in a generic monospace fallback.

## Layout

Layout is a character grid: 40 columns by 25 rows, an 8px sub-step, 16px line-height, and spacing tokens of 8/16/24/32/40px. The screen sits inside the border frame with 24–40px of bright blue visible on all sides, and content inside the field starts after a header line and stops before the status and prompt line. Columns are fixed and labels align on a colon rule — 'DEVICE 8:  OK' — rather than flexbox gaps. Nothing floats: panels butt against cell edges behind a 4px light-blue frame, and the smallest gutter in the system is one cell.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth is attribute-based, not lit: a raised panel is a 4px #a0a0ff frame with a reverse-video title run and at most a hard 8px 8px 0 #000066 offset, and a modal is simply an inverse-video block. Selection, hover, focus, and pressed states invert the foreground/background pair, which is the only 'lift' the hardware could express. The single tolerance is the 0 0 6px #8080ff glow, reserved for a focused field or a live readout, never for containers. Blurred drop shadows, bevels, and glass effects do not exist in this system.

## Shapes

Every shape is a square-cornered rectangle on the cell grid: radius 0, 4px borders, 8px block cursors, and rules made of repeated '=' or '-' glyph runs. Icons and imagery are PETSCII or pixel art rendered with image-rendering: pixelated at integer 2x/3x/4x scale so no anti-aliased edge appears. Circles do not exist except as block-stacked approximations, and corners are chamfered by removing whole 8px blocks rather than rounding them.

## Components

Panels are 4px light-blue frames with a reverse-video title; buttons are light-blue plates (#a0a0ff on ink #0000aa) with a bright blue border and a hard offset that translates away on :active; inputs are inset blue wells with a #3f3fff border and a hand-drawn 8x16 block caret. The status bar is a 24px full-inverse strip that reads 'READY. 38911 BASIC BYTES FREE DEVICE 8: OK', the menu is a vertical list whose selected row is inverse video with a blinking '>' in the gutter, and errors print as '?SYNTAX ERROR' on the following line instead of recoloring a border.

## Do's and Don'ts

Do keep the border frame around the screen, do invert attribute pairs instead of tinting, do stay on the 8x8 cell, and do let the blink be the only motion. Don't round a corner, don't blur a shadow, don't fade with opacity, don't run the blue field edge-to-edge without its frame, and don't introduce a color that is not one of the sixteen palette entries — the machine had no others.
